This talk introduces the fundamental group as an introduction to algebraic topology. We begin with motivating problems in two dimensions that resist direct topological methods but become tractable once recast algebraically — among them, the Brouwer fixed point theorem (does every continuous self-map of the disk have a fixed point?) and the non-existence of a retraction from the ball onto its boundary sphere.

After defining the fundamental group, we develop covering spaces to calculate the fundamental group, listing key properties of covering spaces and est of its fundamental group. We then discuss further properties of the fundamental group, including its behavior under homotopy equivalence and the extent to which it captures the topology of a space. We look at a more analytical way to calculate the fundamental group using van Kampen's theorem, which computes the fundamental group of a space from those of simpler open pieces covering it, we conclude by applying it to determine the fundamental groups of all orientable surfaces.
