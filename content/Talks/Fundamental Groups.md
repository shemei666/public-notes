This talk introduces the fundamental group as an introduction to algebraic topology. We begin with motivating problems in topology like is there a map from $B^n$ to $B^n$ without any fixed points? Is there a retraction from $B^n \to S^n$, Is $\mathbb{R}^n \cong \mathbb{R}^m$ for $m\neq n$ . These problems are hard to tackle using just point-set topology, using fundamental groups we can resolve these questions for 2 dimensions.

After defining the fundamental group, we develop covering spaces to calculate the fundamental group, listing key properties of covering spaces and discussing its relation to the fundamental group and its subgroups. We then discuss further properties of the fundamental group, including its behavior under homotopy equivalence and the extent to which it captures the topology of a space. We look at a more analytical way to calculate the fundamental group using van Kampen's theorem, which computes the fundamental group of a space from those of simpler open pieces covering it, we conclude by applying it to determine the fundamental groups of all orientable surfaces.
