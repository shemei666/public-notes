This talk introduces the fundamental group as an introduction to algebraic topology. We begin with motivating problems in two dimensions that resist direct topological methods but become tractable once recast algebraically — among them, the Brouwer fixed point theorem (does every continuous self-map of the disk have a fixed point?) and the non-existence of a retraction from the ball onto its boundary sphere.

After defining the fundamental group, we develop covering space theory as a primary computational technique, listing key properties of covering spaces and establishing the correspondence between coverings of a space and subgroups of its fundamental group, with particular attention to the role of the number of sheets. We then discuss further properties of the fundamental group, including its behavior under homotopy equivalence and the extent to which it captures the topology of a space. The talk concludes with van Kampen's theorem, which computes the fundamental group of a space from those of simpler open pieces covering it, applied here to determine the fundamental groups of all orientable surfaces.
